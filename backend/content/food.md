# Food safety when the power is out
category: Food
source: Based on USDA food safety guidance

- Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as possible.
- A closed refrigerator keeps food safe for about 4 hours.
- A full freezer keeps food frozen for about 48 hours (24 hours if half full).
- Eat perishable food first (meat, fish, milk, leftovers), then fridge food, then frozen food, then canned and dry goods.
- Throw away meat, fish, eggs, milk and leftovers that have been above 4 °C for more than 2 hours.
- When in doubt, throw it out. Never taste food to check if it is safe.

# Food after a flood
category: Food
source: Based on USDA / WHO flood food safety guidance

Throw away any food that touched flood water, including food in plastic, cardboard, screw-top jars and sacks of rice, because flood water carries sewage and chemicals.

Commercially sealed metal cans that are not damaged can be saved: remove labels, wash with soap and water, soak for 15 minutes in a solution of about 3 tablespoons of unscented bleach per 4 liters of water (or boil the cans for 2 minutes), then relabel with a marker.

Wash and disinfect dishes and cooking utensils before use.

# Cooking with little fuel
category: Food
source: CollapseAI Essentials

- Soak rice and beans before cooking to cut cooking time.
- Use a lid. It saves a lot of fuel.
- Cut food into small pieces.
- Retained-heat ("hay box") cooking: bring food to a boil for a few minutes, then wrap the covered pot tightly in blankets, towels or put it in a box filled with dry grass or crumpled paper. It keeps cooking for hours without fuel.
- Cook outdoors or in a well-ventilated area. Charcoal stoves indoors can kill with carbon monoxide.

# Fast-growing food crops (Philippines)
category: Farming
source: CollapseAI Essentials (based on common Philippine home gardening practice)

Fast crops for an emergency garden:
- Kangkong (water spinach): grows from cuttings in wet soil or water; harvest in about 3–4 weeks.
- Camote (sweet potato): plant vine cuttings; tops can be eaten in weeks, roots in about 3–4 months.
- Pechay: harvest in about 4–6 weeks.
- Malunggay (moringa): plant thick stem cuttings; leaves are very nutritious and grow back after picking.
- Mung beans (monggo): sprouts in a few days without soil; plants give beans in about 2 months.
- Okra, tomatoes, eggplant: about 2–3 months.

Tips: use containers or sacks if land is flooded or limited; compost kitchen scraps; water early in the morning; save seeds from the best plants.

# Preserving food without a fridge
category: Food
source: Based on FAO food preservation guidance

- Drying: slice fish, meat, fruit or vegetables thinly and dry in the sun on a raised rack, covered with mesh against flies. Dry until brittle or leathery.
- Salting: pack fish or meat in plenty of salt (as in daing or tuyo), then dry.
- Smoking: hang thin strips above a low, smoky fire for many hours.
- Pickling: vinegar with salt (achara, paksiw) slows spoilage.
- Store dried food in sealed, dry containers away from rats and insects.
