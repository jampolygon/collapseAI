# Fever
category: Health
source: Based on WHO / DOH guidance

Fever is the body fighting an infection. A temperature of 38 °C or higher is a fever.

Home care:
- Rest and drink plenty of fluids (water, ORS, soup).
- Wear light clothing; sponge with lukewarm (not cold) water.
- Paracetamol can lower fever and pain. Follow the dose on the package for the person's age and weight. Do not exceed the daily maximum.
- If dengue is possible, do NOT give aspirin or ibuprofen (they increase bleeding risk).

Get help urgently if: stiff neck, confusion or very sleepy, seizure, difficulty breathing, rash that does not fade when pressed, fever in a baby under 3 months, fever lasting more than 3 days, signs of dehydration, or bleeding.

# Diarrhea
category: Health
source: Based on WHO diarrhea treatment guidance

The main danger is dehydration, especially for children.

1. Give oral rehydration solution (ORS) after every loose stool. Homemade ORS: 1 liter safe water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + 1/2 level teaspoon salt.
2. Keep eating. Continue breastfeeding babies.
3. For children, health workers give zinc for 10–14 days; it shortens the illness.
4. Wash hands with soap after using the toilet and before handling food.

Get help if: blood in the stool, cannot drink, repeated vomiting, very sleepy, sunken eyes, no urine for 6 hours or more, high fever, or diarrhea lasting more than 2 weeks.

# Dengue
category: Health
source: Based on WHO / DOH dengue guidance

Dengue spreads by Aedes mosquitoes, which bite during the day. It is common in the rainy season.

Symptoms: sudden high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, rash, nausea.

Warning signs (go to a hospital immediately): severe stomach pain, repeated vomiting, bleeding gums or nose, blood in vomit or stool, very tired or restless, fast breathing, cold clammy skin. These often appear when the fever goes down (day 3–7), which is the most dangerous time.

Care: rest, drink lots of fluids, paracetamol for fever (follow the package). Never give aspirin or ibuprofen.

Prevention: remove standing water (old tires, cans, flower pots), cover water containers, use mosquito nets and repellent, wear long sleeves.

# Leptospirosis
category: Health
source: Based on DOH Philippines leptospirosis guidance

Leptospirosis is a bacterial infection from water or mud contaminated by the urine of infected animals, mostly rats. It is common after floods.

Symptoms appear 2 to 30 days after exposure: sudden fever, headache, muscle pain (especially calves), red eyes, chills. Severe cases: yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, little urine, bleeding, difficulty breathing.

What to do:
- If you waded in flood water and get a fever, see a doctor or health center immediately and tell them about the flood exposure. Early antibiotics work well.
- Doctors may give preventive antibiotics to people with flood exposure; take only as prescribed.

Prevention: avoid wading in flood water, wear boots, cover cuts, wash with soap and clean water after exposure, control rats.

# Wound care and infection
category: Health
source: Based on WHO wound care guidance

1. Wash your hands. Clean the wound with plenty of clean (boiled and cooled) water. Remove dirt gently.
2. Apply an antiseptic such as povidone-iodine if available.
3. Cover with a clean dressing. Change it daily or when wet or dirty.
4. Deep, dirty or puncture wounds (rusty nails, animal bites) carry a risk of tetanus. A tetanus shot is needed if not vaccinated in the last 5–10 years.

Signs of infection: increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pain, pus, red streaks spreading from the wound, fever. Get medical help; antibiotics may be needed. Red streaks or fever mean the infection is spreading. That is urgent.

# Hygiene and sanitation
category: Health
source: Based on WHO WASH guidance

- Wash hands with soap and water for 20 seconds: after the toilet, before cooking and eating, after handling waste or animals.
- If there is no soap, ash or sand can be used to scrub, then rinse.
- Build latrines at least 30 meters from any water source and downhill from it.
- Bury or burn garbage away from water and living areas.
- Keep drinking water covered and use a clean cup or tap to take it out, not hands.
- Wash and cook food well; eat it while hot.
